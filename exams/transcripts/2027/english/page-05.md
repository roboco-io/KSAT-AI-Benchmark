People show a tendency to trust ideas that correspond to their beliefs, even though there is little or no objective evidence to support them. Such ideas may eventually turn out to be false, if the assumptions underlying them ① <u>largely</u> rely on subjective observations. Careful thinkers thus try to test their existing beliefs, rather than simply maintaining them. When new evidence does not support the idea that they have entertained, they reexamine ② <u>it</u> from alternative viewpoints. This inspires them to go about challenging the problematic belief. Therefore, ③ <u>think</u> reflectively about the existing beliefs helps people adjust them according to available evidence. Furthermore, it enables people to become less likely to accept new ideas just because they are tempting or consistent with ④ <u>what</u> they have long believed. Although individuals differ considerably in their belief systems, those who objectify and test their own beliefs tend to think more critically than those who ⑤ <u>do</u> not.

30. 다음 글의 밑줄 친 부분 중, 문맥상 낱말의 쓰임이 적절하지 <u>않은</u> 것은? [3점]

Some authors believe that *collectives* of individuals can be more intelligent than individuals taken singly, sometimes pointing to the example of ants, bees, and other social animals. It is certainly true that, by addressing a question to a collective (crowd intelligence), you may get a good answer more ① <u>easily</u> than by, say, researching the literature or asking a philosopher. But this works in a ② <u>reliable</u> fashion only where some genuine expert happens to be in the crowd. This is because human intelligence is an individual capability that is ③ <u>additive</u>. Collectives can, certainly, carry out plans that individuals cannot — such as building Cologne Cathedral. Collectives can also evolve new social patterns over very long periods of time through trial-and-error or through the sort of transgenerational ④ <u>collaboration</u> that is involved, for example, in the creation and sustainment of scientific disciplines. But collectives do not write poetry or novels, discover new aspects of nature, or solve difficult mathematical problems. This is done predominantly by ⑤ <u>individuals</u> or by very small groups.
